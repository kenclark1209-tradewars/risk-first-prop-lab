FTMO Review 2026: Rules, XAUUSD Costs, Payouts & Verdict

The benchmark firm, audited the Lab way: every challenge rule with its calculation basis, every fee including the gold commission formula, payout terms in full — and a clear line between what FTMO publishes and what the Lab has actually measured.

By Thang Nguyen · FTMO Prime TraderPublished 18 Nov 2026Rules & prices checked 18 Nov 2026Series: Prop Lab #001Last updated 18 Nov 2026Next review 18 Dec 2026
Verdict — interim

The most auditable rulebook in the industry, and the firm this Lab benchmarks against — but the Lab's own payout proof is still pending. FTMO's published terms are complete enough to verify line by line, its 2-Step structure (5% daily / 10% static max loss) is the friendliest mainstream setup for gold, and its fee is refunded with the first payout. What keeps the score provisional is simple: on this site, payout points are earned by a completed, timed Lab payout — not by reputation.

Risk First Score: 7 / 10 criteria verifiable from public documents · Payout speed, payout reliability and measured gold cost pending Lab test · how scoring works

Overview

FTMO is a Prague-based prop firm running trader evaluations since 2015 — one of the oldest names in retail prop trading. Traders buy an evaluation, trade a simulated account on MT4, MT5, cTrader or TradingView, and on passing receive an FTMO Account that pays a share of simulated profits as a reward. In 2026 the CFD line-up has three products: the classic 2-Step Challenge (Challenge + Verification), a 1-Step Challenge, and an Instant Account with no evaluation. A separate FTMO Futures track exists with different rules and a different restricted-country list — this review covers the CFD products only. Account sizes run from $10K to $200K, priced in euros.

This review focuses on the 2-Step, the product most gold traders buy, and flags where the 1-Step and Instant differ — because they differ exactly where it hurts: drawdown type and consistency rules.

Pros & cons

Strengths

  • +Static 10% max loss on the 2-Step — the floor never trails, so banked profit becomes cushion. The friendliest mainstream structure for gold.
  • +Fee refunded with the first payout, no time limit, no consistency rule on the 2-Step, and a decade of operating history since 2015.
  • +Transparent gold pricing: live spreads on a public symbols page and a published commission formula — rare in this industry.
  • +Vietnam (CFD) and US access: Vietnam is not on the CFD restricted list; US traders are served via the FTMO US / OANDA route. Futures is a different list — see Country Check.

Watch-outs

  • −The daily loss is equity-based. Floating gold losses count intraday — the exact trap in the Rule Trap audit. Size from equity, not balance.
  • −Funded Standard restricts news and weekend holding (±2 minutes on listed releases; flat before the weekend). News and swing traders need the Swing account type — with lower 1:30 leverage.
  • −The 1-Step and Instant are different products wearing the same logo: trailing max loss and best-day rules make them materially harder than the 2-Step.
  • −Premium pricing. At list price the $100K 2-Step (€540) costs more than most rivals; the value case rests on the refund and the rule structure, not the sticker.

Who should use FTMO — and who should not

  • Good fit: gold and FX traders who want a static drawdown floor, no deadline pressure, and a refundable fee — and who can size positions against an equity-based daily limit.
  • Good fit: Vietnamese CFD traders (residency-based check still required before buying) and US traders via the OANDA route.
  • Poor fit: news traders on the Standard funded account, weekend swing traders who refuse the Swing account's lower leverage, and anyone who needs the cheapest possible entry fee.

Verdict

Risk First verdict: the reference standard — provisionally. On published terms alone, FTMO's 2-Step is the cleanest mainstream offer for a gold trader: static max loss, refundable fee, no consistency rule, transparent commissions. The Lab's final score waits for the two things paper cannot prove: a measured all-in gold cost from the MT5 log and a timed, completed payout. Until then it leads the Directory on documentation, not on proof. Compare it head-to-head in Prop Firm Match.

Rule Trap: Daily Drawdown — Balance-Based, Equity-Based, or Highest-of-Both?

More challenges die from this one definition than from bad trading. Three mainstream calculation methods exist, they demand three different position sizes, and the difference is invisible until the day gold spikes against you. This audit works all three in dollars, then maps every reviewed firm to its method.

By Thang Nguyen · FTMO Prime TraderPublished 18 Nov 2026Firm methods checked 18 Nov 2026Series: Rule Trap Audit #001Last updated 18 Nov 2026Next review 18 Dec 2026

The three methods

  • Balance-based: the daily limit is measured against your balance at the start of the day (or a fixed % of the initial balance). Floating losses on open trades do not move the baseline — the limit bites on realised results and the equity floor beneath them.
  • Equity-based: the limit follows live equity including unrealised P&L. A gold spike against an open position can breach the limit mid-trade — the account fails while the trade idea is still valid.
  • Highest-of-both (hybrid): the baseline is the higher of your opening balance or opening equity. Yesterday's floating profit raises today's baseline, so a normal pullback after a good day can breach a limit you “had room” for.

None of the three is fraudulent. All three are published by the firms that use them. The trap is assuming the one you are used to — and sizing for it — at a firm that uses another.

Worked example: one $100K account, one bad gold morning

Setup, identical in all three worlds: $100,000 account, 5% daily loss limit, you closed yesterday at $101,000 balance with no open trades, and this morning you are long gold. By 10:00 your open position is floating −$4,200; by 11:00 it recovers and closes at +$600.

Why gold traders get caught first

XAUUSD routinely moves $20–$40 in minutes around London and New York data releases — $2,000–$4,000 per lot. On an equity-based or highest-of-both limit, that intraday swing, not your closed result, decides whether the challenge survives the day. A 1-lot gold position with a $30 stop carries $3,000 of intraday risk: that is 60% of a $100K account's 5% daily allowance before slippage, and more than 100% of a 3% allowance. News traders and swing traders holding through releases are exposed the most — and at two of the six firms (FTMO Standard funded, Funding Pips funded) the release window itself is separately restricted, so the trap has a second door.

The sizing rule that survives all three methods

  • Size from equity, always. Treat your daily budget as an equity budget even at a balance-based firm; you will simply be safer than required.
  • Risk per trade ≤ ⅓ of the daily limit. On a 5% daily limit, cap single-trade risk at ~1.5% and total open risk at ~2.5%, leaving room for a second idea and for slippage.
  • Convert gold stops to dollars before lots: risk $ = stop distance in dollars × 100 × lots (1 lot = 100 oz). If the dollar figure surprises you, the lot size is wrong.
  • After a winning day at a highest-of-both firm, cut size. Your baseline — and therefore your floor — just moved up. Yesterday's profit is today's tighter limit.
  • At a trailing-max firm, banked profit is not cushion. The floor follows closed profits up (E8 One locks at the initial balance). Trade the distance to the floor, not the account balance.

The 60-second check before you buy any challenge

  • Search the firm's terms for “daily” and read whether the reference is balance, equity, or “whichever is higher” — and whether the % is of the initial balance or the day's baseline.
  • Check the reset time and timezone (midnight Prague at FTMO; 00:00 UTC+3 at Funding Pips; server time elsewhere) — a “day” that resets at 05:00 your time splits your session in two.
  • Check the max loss separately: static, end-of-day trailing, intraday trailing, or dynamic-on-closed-balance. It is a different clause from the daily limit and firms mix them freely (Alpha: static Pro, trailing One).
  • Check whether the variant changes the basis — Alpha Pro 6% and 8% use different daily bases at the same firm.
  • Screenshot the clauses with the date. If the firm later changes them, that screenshot is your only evidence.

How country bans actually work

  • Residency vs citizenship are different tests. Most firms ban by residency (where you live and KYC from). Some — FundedNext CFD is the strict example in this set — ban residents and citizens, so a Vietnamese passport holder living abroad is still barred there. Read which test your firm applies before assuming an overseas address fixes it.
  • Products at the same firm have different lists. FTMO CFD leaves Vietnam eligible while FTMO Futures bars Vietnamese residents. “FTMO accepts Vietnam” is only ever true with a product name attached.
  • VPNs do not fix a ban. Bans are enforced at KYC and payout, not at login. FundedNext's terms make VPN bypass an immediate, permanent termination — the standard outcome across the industry is a closed account and no payout.
  • Travel is not residency — usually. Some firms restrict actions (buying, resetting, requesting payout) while you are physically in a restricted country even if you live elsewhere. If you travel, check the clause or ask support in writing first.
  • Restrictions come from sanctions law, payment rails and fraud patterns — not from the firm's opinion of your trading. That is why lists change quietly when payment partners change.

2 · The residency-vs-citizenship trap

Most traders assume a ban is about where they sit. It is usually about where they live (residency, proven by KYC documents), sometimes about the passport they hold (citizenship), and at FundedNext CFD about both at once. Practical consequences:

  • A Vietnamese resident cannot “fix” a residency ban with a VPN, a foreign VPS or a friend's address — KYC at payout compares your documents with the clause, and the account closes there.
  • A Vietnamese citizen legally resident abroad is fine at residency-based firms, but still barred at FundedNext CFD, whose clause names citizens explicitly.
  • Travelling through Vietnam is a third case: some firms restrict buying, resetting or withdrawing while you are physically in a restricted country. If you travel, notify support before the trip and keep the reply.

4 · Tax and paperwork: prepare from day one

  • Payouts are income. Money received from a foreign firm is income you should declare for personal income tax in Vietnam under current rules. The Lab does not give tax advice — when amounts become material, ask an accountant or your local tax authority and keep the answer in writing.
  • Keep a complete file per payout: the firm's terms/agreement, the payout statement, bank statements or wallet transaction history, and a dated screenshot of the country clause from the day you bought the challenge.
  • Never receive payouts into someone else's account. A name mismatch with KYC risks refusal by the firm and makes the source of funds unexplainable to your bank and the tax authority.
  • Challenge fees are part of your records too: keep purchase invoices — for refund claims and as the cost side of your trading activity file.

5 · The four mistakes that cost Vietnamese traders the most

  • Buying first, reading the restricted list second. The most expensive and most avoidable mistake — run section 1 of this page and Country Check before paying anything.
  • Using a VPN to “get around” a country ban. Bans are enforced on residency and KYC, not IP. It works at purchase and fails at payout — the worst possible time.
  • Choosing a firm for its advertised payout speed without checking the rail. “Paid in 24 hours” is meaningless if the rail cannot land in Vietnam. Filter by country first in Prop Firm Match.
  • Keeping no paperwork. When the bank asks about the source of funds, old emails are archaeology — firms change terms, rails and dashboards. Build the file as you go (section 4).

Formulas & assumptions — read before you trade

          
    
            
  • Expectancy (R) = win rate × RRR − (1 − win rate) — the average R earned per trade. At 60% and 1:1.5 that is 0.60 × 1.5 − 0.40 = +0.50R.
    • 
            
  • Risk per trade = daily profit target ÷ (trades per day × expectancy R) — sized so the plan hits the target in expectation, not on a guaranteed path.
    • 
            
  • Lot size = risk per trade ÷ (stop-loss distance × $ value per unit per lot). XAUUSD: 1 lot = 100 oz, so $1.00 of price movement = $100 per lot. EURUSD / GBPUSD: $10 per pip per lot. USDJPY / GBPJPY: ≈ $6.70 per pip per lot at a rate near 150 — verify on your broker. BTCUSD: assumes 1 lot = 1 BTC — verify the contract size.
    • 
            
  • Daily loss budget is computed on the initial balance. Some firms fix the daily limit on the initial balance, others test live equity against a midnight balance — the basis changes how much room you really have. Check the basis for your firm in its review and in the Daily Drawdown Rule Trap audit.
    • 
            
  • The plan spreads the target evenly across days and ignores spread, commission, swap and slippage — your real all-in cost lowers the effective expectancy. Gold cost per firm is tracked in each review; where the Lab has not measured it yet it stays Awaiting measurement.
    • 
            
  • Expectancy is an average over many trades. A losing streak can breach the daily or max loss limit long before the average asserts itself — that is exactly what the warnings above test.
    • 
            
  • This planner does not promise or imply a pass. It tests whether a plan is arithmetically compatible with a firm's rules. Most traders fail challenges; a plan that only works with no losing streak is not a plan.
    •

    METHOD
    How the simulation works
    
      
    
      
    Every formula and assumption is on the table. The rule values come from the same Lab rule table as the Challenge Pass Planner — each firm's published terms, checked 18 Nov 2026. Where a rule is marked Unverified in that data, the simulator shows it, labels it, and does not enforce it.
    
      
    
        
      
          
    • Daily loss — equity-based firms (basis text says live equity / floating losses count, e.g. FTMO 2-Step): daily floor = start-of-day balance − daily% × initial balance. The day's worst equity is start-of-day balance − worst floating dip you entered. If worst equity falls below the floor at any moment, the account is closed that day — even if the day later closes in profit.
      • 
          
    • Daily loss — balance-based firms: only the closed day counts. The account breaches if closed P/L < −(daily% × initial balance). The floating dip is still shown, but it cannot close the account under this basis.
      • 
          
    • Daily loss — highest-of-both firms (Blue Guardian 2-Step and 1-Step Standard): daily floor = max(balance, equity) at the 5:00 PM New York reset − daily% × starting balance. Verified against the Blue Guardian Help Centre (2-Step Standard rules, updated 13 Nov 2026; checked 18 Nov 2026) — its own worked example: $100K with $2,000 floating profit → equity $102,000 → floor $98,000. Built-in check: starting balance $100,000, balance at reset $100,000, equity at reset $103,000 → floor $99,000, and equity of $98,000 breaches by $1,000. If you do not enter a day's equity at reset, that day's daily rule reports Insufficient data — no floor is guessed.
      • 
          
    • Daily loss — soft limits: where the published rule pauses the session instead of failing the account (Apex EOD plans), the simulator reports a pause, not a closure. Models with no daily limit (MyFundedFutures Rapid, Topstep Combine default) show “No daily loss limit”.
      • 
          
    • Max loss — static: floor = initial balance − max% × initial balance, tested against both worst intraday equity and the closing balance. End-of-day trailing: floor = highest end-of-day balance so far − max% × initial balance, tested at the close (Topstep's limit is published as enforced in real time on unrealised P&L, so the intraday dip is tested too). Dynamic trailing: floor = highest balance reached (intraday, approximated by the best closing balance so far) − max% × initial balance, tested against worst intraday equity.
      • 
          
    • Profit target: reached on the first day the closing balance reaches initial balance × (1 + target%). Phase 1 is simulated; where the model has a Phase 2 target it is shown in the rules panel, not simulated.
      • 
          
    • Best-day / consistency cap: at the end of the log, if the model has a cap and the best single day's profit is more than cap% × total profit, the phase fails the consistency rule — the day and both numbers are named. Topstep's cap is measured against the profit target instead, and breaching it raises the target rather than failing the account, per its published rule. Models with no cap say so.
      • 
          
    • Minimum trading days: days with any entry in the log count as traded days. If the target is reached in fewer days than the model's minimum, the verdict reads “target reached but min-days not met”.
      • 
          
    • News & weekend flags: if you tick news trading or weekend holding on a model whose published rules restrict it, the day is flagged “rule violation — account at risk / flagged”. These are warnings, not automatic closures, unless the firm's published rule says closure — the flag names the rule text so you can judge.
      • 
          
    • Approximations, stated plainly: intraday equity is approximated from the single worst floating dip you enter per day — the simulator cannot see real tick data, the order of the dip versus the day's trades, spread widening, slippage, commissions or swap. One dip number per day is a simplification of a curve. Best-day cap treatment follows the firm's published rule as recorded in the Lab rule table.
      • 
          
    • Results are educational. Surviving this simulation does not mean you will pass the real challenge, and dying here does not mean you would have — it means the shape of those days collides with the published rules. Rules change by announcement; re-confirm on the firm's site before buying.
      • 
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    What this tool cannot do
    
        
    It cannot see your broker's tick data, so it cannot replay what your equity actually did minute by minute — only the worst dip you tell it. It does not model spread, commission, slippage, swap, partial fills, or a firm's discretionary reviews (risk-team audits, “gambling” or hedging clauses, payout-stage re-checks like retrospective per-trade risk rules). It simulates the challenge phase only — funded-stage rules (payout buffers, weekend/news bans that start at funding, drawdown switches) are named in each firm's review but are not enforced here. And where the Lab could not verify a rule on an official page, the simulator refuses to guess: the rule is shown as Not verified and left unenforced.

    Formulas & assumptions — read before you believe any number here
    
          
      
            
    • Expectancy per trade E = win rate × RRR − (1 − win rate) × 1, in R units (average win = RRR × R, average loss = 1R). At 60% and 1:1.5 that is 0.60 × 1.5 − 0.40 = +0.50R. If E ≤ 0 you have no positive expectancy — the page shows that as its own warning, separate from the probability: no challenge price is cheap enough to fix a missing edge, but the warning never replaces the number.
      • 
            
    • Model pass probability (one attempt) = (1 − e^(−2μd/σ²)) ÷ (1 − e^(−2μ(d+t)/σ²)) — a diffusion (Brownian) approximation of your trade-by-trade random walk with drift, where μ = E, σ² = win rate × (RRR − μ)² + (1 − win rate) × (−1 − μ)², d = max drawdown % ÷ risk per trade % and t = profit target % ÷ risk per trade %. At μ = 0 the formula itself is 0/0, so the tool uses its zero-drift limit P = d ÷ (d + t) — the starting point's fractional distance between the barriers. Check it on the symmetric case: one phase, barriers ±10%, each trade ±1% with equal probability, no time limit → d = t = 10, so P = 10 ÷ 20 = 50% (discrete gambler's ruin agrees). A driftless walk is a coin flip between the barriers, not a ~0% chance. For two-step models the attempt probability is the product of the two phase probabilities — you must survive both barriers in sequence, on the same max-drawdown room. Where a case is not estimated at all, this page shows Not estimated in the main result, the attempts, the cost and the comparison table — the same words in all four places.
      • 
            
    • Expected attempts = 1 ÷ P · Expected total cost = price × expected attempts · Expected remaining cost = price × max(0, expected attempts − attempts already bought) · Sunk cost = price × attempts already bought.
      • 
            
    • This pass probability is a model estimate built from your own win rate and RRR — it is not the firm's real pass rate, and no firm publishes one you can audit. Treat it as an upper bound, not a forecast.
      • 
            
    • The model is optimistic by construction. It ignores the daily drawdown limit, consistency and best-day caps, news and weekend rules, minimum trading days, spread, slippage and psychology — every one of those can only push your real probability below the number shown. Test the rules that actually close accounts in the Rule Trap Simulator, and build the day-by-day plan in the Challenge Pass Planner.
      • 
            
    • Prices, profit targets and max drawdowns come from the same Lab rule table as the Planner (each firm's published terms, checked 18 Nov 2026). Where the Lab holds no price for a firm and size, the price field stays empty and is marked Unverified — the Lab does not invent prices; enter the figure from the firm's own checkout.
      • 
            
    • If the firm changes the price at checkout — a discount, an add-on, a different tier — re-enter the checkout price; every result on this page follows the number in the price field.
      • 
            
    • No pass is promised or implied. An expectation is an average over many attempts, not a schedule: you can fail more times in a row than the average suggests, and the expected cost is money genuinely at risk of being spent in full.

    REALITY
    Why the sticker price lies
    
      
    
      
    A challenge fee is priced like a product and sold like a lottery ticket. The number on the pricing page is the cost of one attempt — but a funded account is what you are actually buying, and the two prices are only equal if you pass first time.
    
      
    
        
    Work it through with the most-bought challenge in the industry. A $100K FTMO 2-Step costs €540. At the industry pass rate of ~14% (FPFX dataset, ~300,000 accounts), the expected number of attempts before one trader gets funded is 1 ÷ 0.14 ≈ 7.1 — so the expected cost of getting funded is €540 × 7.1 ≈ €3,857. Not €540. And that is before resets, add-ons, and the months of trading time those seven attempts take.
    
        
    Three things follow. First, comparing firms on sticker price alone is meaningless: a €540 challenge you pass one time in seven costs more than a €700 challenge whose drawdown rules fit your strategy well enough to pass one time in three. Second, the cheapest way to cut the true cost is not a discount code — it is raising the per-attempt pass probability: a drawdown model that fits your losing streaks, and risk per trade small enough that the max-loss barrier sits many trades away. Third, money already spent on failed attempts is gone — it is a sunk cost, and it should never be the reason you buy the next attempt. This page keeps it in its own box for exactly that reason.
    
        
    Run your own numbers above. If the expected total cost surprises you, that surprise is the point: the sticker price was never the price of a funded account.

    Breakout Prop runs the simplest rulebook in Batch 2: one-step evaluations, a static drawdown that never trails, no consistency rule, no minimum days, news trading allowed, and on-demand USDC payouts from the day you are funded. It is also the only firm in this batch whose current restricted list does not name Vietnam. The audit covers the catches: crypto-only payouts, a separate BVI funded entity, and a 3% Turbo drawdown that gives you exactly one bad day.
    
      
    By Thang Nguyen · FTMO Prime TraderPublished 18 Nov 2026Rules & prices checked 18 Nov 2026Series: Prop Lab #013Last updated 18 Nov 2026Next review 18 Dec 2026
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Verdict — interim
    
        
    The cleanest rules in the batch, paid out in crypto only. Static drawdown, zero consistency traps, same-day funding and a Kraken parent (acquired Sep 2025) make Breakout the easiest firm here to understand — and one of only two in Batch 2 a Vietnamese trader can actually buy today. The range (7.0–8.0) is capped by USDC-only payouts with wallet and gas risk, the brutal Turbo maths (3% target room = 3% daily limit), user-reported execution slippage, and a funded contract held with a separate BVI company. One honesty note: Breakout's homepage claims a 4.7 Trustpilot rating; Trustpilot's own page shows 4.6/5 across 1,307 reviews — this review uses Trustpilot's figure.
    
        
    Risk First Score: 7.5 / 10 — midpoint of the proposed 7.0–8.0 range from the 18 Nov 2026 research dossier · not a final Lab score · Payout points pending Lab test · Vietnam: not on the current restricted list — re-verify at checkout
    
      
    

      
    Overview
    
      
    Breakout Prop has operated since November 2023. Evaluations are sold by Breakout Trading Group, LLC (Delaware); funded accounts are contracted with Payward Oceanic Ltd (“POL”, British Virgin Islands) — part of the Kraken group, which acquired Breakout in September 2025. Trading runs on the firm's own Breakout Terminal (there is no MT5), across crypto and real-world-asset markets (gold, Brent, copper, natural gas, indices). The homepage claims $80M+ paid to traders since launch — a firm figure, not independently verified — and publishes a public payout leaderboard. Funded accounts remain simulated; the firm discloses that POL may internalise your trade ideas rather than routing them to a market.

    Rules: hard or easy?
    
      
    The easiest rulebook in Batch 2 — with plan maths that range from fair to savage. Classic (10% target against 6% static) is a reasonable one-step. Turbo (9% against 3%, with a 3% daily limit) is a coin-flip dressed as a discount: your entire drawdown is one daily limit. There are no consistency, news, holding or minimum-day traps anywhere — difficulty here is purely the target-to-drawdown ratio you choose.
